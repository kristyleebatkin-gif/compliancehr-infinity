# NDIS reportable incidents

Last checked: 2 November 2026. Applies to registered NDIS providers.
Sources: NDIS Commission "Reportable incidents: Detailed guidance for registered NDIS providers"; NDIS (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules 2018 (legislation.gov.au F2018L00633); ndiscommission.gov.au reportable incidents pages.

## What counts as a reportable incident
An incident (or an allegation of one) that occurred, or is alleged to have occurred, in connection with the provision of NDIS supports or services, and is one of:
1. The death of a person with disability.
2. Serious injury of a person with disability.
3. Abuse or neglect of a person with disability.
4. Unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of, a person with disability.
5. Sexual misconduct committed against, or in the presence of, a person with disability, including grooming for sexual activity.
6. The use of a restrictive practice on a person with disability that is unauthorised or not in accordance with their behaviour support plan.

Allegations count. Providers do not need proof before notifying.

## Timeframes (from when key personnel become aware)
- **Immediate notification: within 24 hours** of key personnel becoming aware of the incident or allegation.
- **Five-business-day report:** further details within 5 business days of key personnel becoming aware.
- **Final report:** if the Commission asks for one, usually within 60 business days of the initial notification. The Commission can change this, for example during a police investigation.
- **Exception - unauthorised restrictive practice:** if it did NOT cause harm to the person, notification is generally due within 5 business days. If it caused harm, the 24-hour deadline applies.
- Business days exclude weekends and public holidays.

In ComplianceHR the clock is set by the incident's "Provider Awareness Date and Time".

## What the provider should also do
- Make sure the person is safe and gets any medical help they need.
- Contact police where a crime may have occurred.
- Tell the participant (or their nominee) unless doing so would put them at risk; record when ("Participant or Nominee Notified").
- Preserve evidence and records.
- Decide whether an internal investigation is needed and record the outcome.
- Review the risk assessment, behaviour support plan and support plan.
- Record the reportability decision and reasons, even when the decision is "not reportable".
- States and territories may have extra reporting (for example a Senior Practitioner for restrictive practices).

## How to answer
- Map the facts to the six types above. If the details are not enough, say what is missing rather than guessing.
- Give the deadline as a rule ("within 24 hours of key personnel becoming aware"), and point to the incident's calculated due date in the app.
- Recommend checking the NDIS Commission website or calling the Commission when unsure.
- Never give legal advice and never say a provider is compliant.
