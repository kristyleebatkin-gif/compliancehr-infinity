# NDIS worker screening (registered providers)

Last checked: 2 November 2026.
Source: NDIS Commission "Worker screening requirements (registered NDIS providers)" and NDIS (Practice Standards - Worker Screening) Rules 2018.

## Risk assessed roles
A role is risk assessed if it:
1. Is a key personnel role (for example CEO or board member); or
2. Involves the direct delivery of specified supports or services to a person with disability; or
3. Is likely to require more than incidental contact with people with disability (for example physical touch, building rapport as a normal part of the role, or contact with multiple people in specialist supports or specialist disability accommodation).

Contact includes physical, face-to-face, verbal, written and electronic contact.
Workers in risk assessed roles need an NDIS Worker Screening Check clearance, unless a transitional or special exception applies under state or territory rules.

## Records the provider must keep
- A list of risk assessed roles: role title, which part of the definition applies, role description, date assessed, and who assessed it. Update it within 20 business days of identifying or reclassifying a role.
- A list of workers in those roles: full name, date of birth, address, roles held, and either clearance details (application reference, check number, expiry date, any suspension or revocation) or the basis for working without a clearance, with start and end dates and any supervisor.
- Records of interim bars, suspensions and exclusions, and what the provider did in response.
- Records of misconduct allegations against cleared workers, investigations and actions.
- Contractor records and the steps taken to confirm their clearances.
- Keep worker screening records for **7 years**. Records must show which workers were in risk assessed roles on any day in the past 7 years.

## Excluded or barred workers
- A worker with an exclusion, interim bar or suspension, or a cancelled application, must not be engaged in a risk assessed role, and exceptions cannot be used for them.
- Check the NDIS Worker Screening Database for excluded workers.
- In some states (for example Queensland) engaging such a person is an offence.

## How to answer
- In ComplianceHR, point to the staff profile, certificates and Workforce Assurance records, and flag clearances that are missing or expiring.
- Rules for interim working arrangements differ by state and territory; recommend checking the local screening unit.
- Never give legal advice.
